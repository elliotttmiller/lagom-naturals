7. Product Education UX

Where product education discusses onset or duration, present it as variable and dependent on the individual, product, dose, food intake, and other factors.

8. Responsible-Use Content

Use responsible-use messaging where appropriate.

Examples of acceptable principles:

Start with a lower serving when unfamiliar with THC.

Allow adequate time before consuming more.

Do not drive or operate machinery after consuming THC.

Keep products away from children and pets.

Do not combine product claims with medical-treatment promises.

Follow local law and product labeling.

Do not invent jurisdiction-specific legal language.

When exact compliance language matters, verify against the applicable statute, regulator, product label, or approved legal copy.

9. Claims & Compliance Guardrails

9.1 Medical Claims

Never write unsupported medical-treatment or cure claims.

Do not write:

"Treats anxiety"

"Cures insomnia"

"Relieves depression"

"Treats chronic pain"

"Prevents disease"

"Clinically proven" unless supported by authorized substantiation

Prefer non-medical experiential or product-description language such as:

"Made for slower evenings"

"A crisp option for social occasions"

"Bright citrus flavor with a clean finish"

"A zero-alcohol alternative for adult occasions"

9.2 Intoxication Claims

Do not glamorize excessive consumption or encourage rapid redosing.

Avoid:

drinking challenges

binge framing

exaggerated psychoactive promises

competitive consumption language

"more is better" messaging

9.3 Age & Audience

The website should be designed for adults of legal purchase age as required by applicable law.

Where an age gate is legally or operationally required:

make it accessible

make it keyboard operable

persist the session appropriately

avoid manipulative or confusing patterns

do not falsely imply legal eligibility based solely on passing a gate

9.4 State-Specific Requirements

Do not assume one nationwide rule set.

Potentially variable areas include:

retail-sale restrictions

When jurisdiction-specific implementation is requested, verify current law before hard-coding compliance logic.

15. TypeScript Domain Contracts

Use strict product contracts.

Example baseline:

export interface CannabinoidAmount {
  name: 'THC' | 'CBD' | 'CBG' | 'CBN' | string;
  amountMg: number;
  scope: 'serving' | 'container' | 'package';
}

export interface BeverageNutrition {
  servingSize?: string;
  calories?: number;
  sugarGrams?: number;
  carbohydrateGrams?: number;
  caffeineMg?: number;
  ingredients?: string[];
  allergens?: string[];
}

export interface LabData {
  batchId?: string;
  testedAt?: string;
  labName?: string;
  coaUrl?: string;
}

export interface BeverageVariant {
  id: string;
  sku?: string;
  label: string;
  canVolumeOz?: number;
  packCount?: number;
  price: number;
  compareAtPrice?: number;
  available: boolean;
}

export interface BeverageProduct {
  id: string;
  slug: string;
  name: string;
  flavor: string;
  flavorFamily?: string;
  description?: string;
  cannabinoids: CannabinoidAmount[];
  nutrition?: BeverageNutrition;
  variants: BeverageVariant[];
  lab?: LabData;
  tags?: string[];
  image: {
    src: string;
    alt: string;
    width?: number;
    height?: number;
  };
}

export interface RetailLocation {
  id: string;
  name: string;
  address: {
    line1?: string;
    city: string;
    region: string;
    postalCode?: string;
    country: string;
  };
  websiteUrl?: string;
  latitude?: number;
  longitude?: number;
}

Extend these interfaces to match real upstream data.

Never invent mandatory fields that the API, CMS, ERP, or commerce source does not provide.
